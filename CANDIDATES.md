# Candidate lines — scoring against the judge's five criteria

Competition: The One-Line Laugh (u-ronigrv-the-one-line-laugh-7ccca46f).
Judge criteria, in order: (1) one funny line ≤ 200 chars; (2) real surprise
at the end that still makes sense; (3) an everyday truth people recognise;
(4) economy — every word earns its place; (5) freshness — not a line heard
many times before. Each scored 1–5. All candidates are original, one
sentence each, 120–200 characters.

## A — Smart fridge (179 chars) — total 19/25

> My smart fridge keeps suggesting recipes based on what's inside it, which
> is mostly condiments and regret, so tonight's dinner is technically a sauce
> with a side of bad decisions.

1. Funny, in limit: 4 — "a sauce with a side of bad decisions" is a solid punch.
2. Surprise at end: 4 — "condiments and regret" twists midway, final clause lands.
3. Everyday truth: 4 — a fridge full of condiments is widely relatable.
4. Economy: 3 — "keeps suggesting recipes based on what's inside it" is wordy.
5. Fresh: 4 — smart fridge + regret pairing is original.

## B — Photo archive (178 chars) — total 21/25

> I finally organized my photos into folders, and now I have a perfectly

2. Surprise at end: 4 — the final clause reframes the whole sentence.

4. Economy: 4 — tight; every clause earns its place.
5. Fresh: 4 — the photo-organising angle is uncommon.

## Selection

B and C tie at 21/25. The judge's own rule decides ties: go through the
criteria in order, let the first difference decide unless a later one
differs by much more. On criterion 4 (economy) C is clearly better — 152
chars, zero waste — while on criterion 5 (freshness) B is better by one
point, not "much more". The competition's tie-break also favours the
shorter text (fewer bytes, then earlier receipt). **C is the entry.**

B is kept as the v2 alternate for the next round if C's round score calls
for a revision.
